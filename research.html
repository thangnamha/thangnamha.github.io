My work sits at the intersection of IR and IPE, asking how the politics between states and firms shapes the international governance of economic security — from critical mineral supply chains to the broader architecture of supply chain risk management.

Western FDI in China-Embedded Critical Mineral Jurisdictions

Dissertation project

This project asks why Western firms invest in critical mineral jurisdictions that are deeply embedded in Chinese-led supply networks, and why the volume and stage of that investment varies so widely across cases. I argue that two factors interact to shape outcomes: a jurisdiction's position in the trade network (a peripheral enclave versus a centralized industrial hub) and its domestic political economy (a developmental versus a rentier coalition). Counterintuitively, deeper embeddedness in a Chinese-led hub can attract rather than deter Western investment where developmental state institutions can credibly commit to diversification. The argument is tested using a country-mineral-year panel, a staggered difference-in-differences design around U.S. Critical Minerals List designations, and paired comparative case studies (Indonesia–Philippines nickel; Chile–Bolivia lithium).

Reengineering Interdependence: State–Firm Politics and International Economic Security Cooperation

Dissertation project

A parallel strand of my dissertation examines how state–firm politics shapes the mode of international cooperation states construct in response to supply chain vulnerabilities. Building on a typology of firm embeddedness in global production networks and state capacity to shape firm behavior, I identify three distinct modes of cooperation — extension, network discipline, and pooled facilitation — and trace them across contemporary cases (U.S. semiconductor and critical minerals policy, EU–Russia energy realignment, Japanese rare earth diversification) alongside historical anchors (COCOM, the IEA, and Allied economic war councils).

Nickel, Coalitions, and the Limits of Resource Nationalism: Indonesia and the Philippines Compared

Standalone paper

A comparative study of Indonesia and the Philippines within the global nickel supply chain, examining how differing domestic coalitions and network positions produced divergent patterns of Chinese and Western investment despite a shared resource base.
